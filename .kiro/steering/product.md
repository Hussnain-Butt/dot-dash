# Dot-Dash — Product

## Purpose

Dot-Dash brings the 1844 telegraph key back as a voice. People who cannot speak and can barely move type with one switch in Morse code, and the browser speaks the message aloud. Free, offline, browser-based. Kiroween 2026 entry — Resurrection category.

## Users

- People who use AAC (augmentative and alternative communication): ALS/MND, cerebral palsy, spinal cord injury, stroke survivors.
- Their carers and family members.

## Must-have features

- **One-switch input**: space bar, large on-screen button, or mouse click. Short press = dot, long press = dash. Pauses end letters and words.
- **Adaptive timing**: learns each person's own dot, dash, and pause lengths. Tolerates slow and uneven presses without misreading.
- **Speech output**: Web Speech API speaks the decoded message aloud.
- **Quick-phrase board**: pre-set urgent phrases such as "I need water" and "I'm in pain".
- **Word prediction**: suggestions from a small built-in word list.
- **Telegraph office look**: high-contrast, spooky-vintage aesthetic.
- **Offline**: works with no network connection.

## Should-have features

- Urdu Morse input and Urdu speech output.
- Practice mode to learn Morse at the user's own pace.
- Carer view: larger text, simpler layout.
- Two-switch mode: one switch for dots, one for dashes.

## Claims we never make

- No medical claims: this is a communication tool, not a medical device or treatment.
- No speed or efficiency claims we have not measured ourselves.
